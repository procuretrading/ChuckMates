Door placard vs sidewall max PSI: which number should you use?

Home » Blog » Door Placard vs Sidewall PSI: Which Tire Pressure to Use

Use the pressure on your vehicle’s Tire and Loading Information placard, usually on the driver’s door jamb, or the one in your owner’s manual. The number molded on the tire sidewall is the tire’s maximum pressure, a limit the tire can safely hold, not a target for everyday driving. Set placard pressure with the tires cold.

What is the pressure on the door placard?

The placard pressure is the vehicle manufacturer’s recommended cold inflation pressure for your vehicle. The Tire and Loading Information placard is a label the manufacturer fixes to the vehicle that lists recommended tire pressures and load information. It usually sits on the driver’s side door jamb. The US Department of Energy’s FuelEconomy.gov notes it can also be in the glove box, and the same number appears in the owner’s manual.

Many placards list different pressures for the front and rear tires, and some list one for the spare. Use the number for each position.

What does the max PSI on the sidewall mean?

The sidewall number is the maximum inflation pressure the tire is built to hold, set by the tire manufacturer. It is a ceiling for that tire in any vehicle, not a recommendation for yours. FuelEconomy.gov is direct about it: “Do not use the maximum pressure printed on the tire’s sidewall.”

Why are the two numbers different?

They answer different questions. The vehicle maker chooses a pressure for your vehicle’s weight, handling, braking, and ride. The tire maker states the most that tire can take, whatever it’s mounted on.

What if your tires aren’t the originals?

If the replacements match the original size and load range, the placard still applies. Load range is the tire’s rated load-carrying class, marked on the sidewall. If you changed tire size or load range, which is common with light-truck tires on lifted trucks or overland builds, the placard may no longer fit. Ask a tire professional, or use the tire manufacturer’s load and inflation tables for your actual vehicle weight.

The same goes for duallys and RVs. Those vehicles depend on load tables, and passenger-car rules of thumb don’t carry over. Our dually guide covers inner-tire access.

How do you check your tires against the placard?

Read the placard, then measure each tire cold with a gauge and adjust it to that number. Step by step:

  1. Note the front, rear, and spare pressures from the placard or owner’s manual.
  2. Remove the valve cap and press the gauge squarely onto the valve stem.
  3. Compare the reading with the placard number for that position.
  4. Add air if it is low. If it is high and the tires are cold, let a little out and measure again.
  5. Replace the cap and repeat on every tire, including the spare if the placard lists one.

Should you check tire pressure hot or cold?

Check cold. The placard number is a cold-tire spec, and NHTSA recommends checking at least once a month, with the tires cold. Pressure rises as tires warm up while driving, so a hot reading will look higher than the true cold number.

Never let air out of hot tires to reach the placard number. When they cool, they will be underinflated. If you have to check warm, wait for the tires to cool, or recheck the next morning.

FAQ

Is it OK to inflate my tires to the max sidewall PSI?

Not for normal driving. The sidewall number is the upper limit. Use the placard pressure unless your vehicle or tire manufacturer specifies otherwise for a particular load.

Why does my placard list different front and rear pressures?

The vehicle manufacturer sets pressure per axle to suit how weight is distributed. Use each number for its position.

What if my vehicle has no placard?

Check the owner’s manual, or ask the dealer or vehicle manufacturer.

Does towing or hauling change the pressure?

It can. Some owner’s manuals list separate pressures for heavy loads or towing, so check yours before you hitch up.
